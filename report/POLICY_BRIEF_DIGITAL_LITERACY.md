# Policy Brief: Institutionalizing Digital Media Literacy in Higher Education

**To:** Academic Council & Directorate of Student Affairs, Central University  
**From:** TruthLens (S-09) Campaign Working Group & Student Journalism Society  
**Date:** September 2026  
**Subject:** Institutional Policy Framework for Combating Digital Misinformation and Enhancing Student Media Literacy

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement
Digital misinformation—ranging from fabricated health advisories and phishing scams to hyper-realistic deepfakes—presents severe academic, financial, and psychological risks to university students. 

Our baseline empirical study across 142 students revealed that **71.8% of undergraduates initially exhibited high susceptibility to online hoaxes**, with over **58.5% relying on encrypted messaging forwards (WhatsApp/Telegram) as primary news conduits**.

The rapid deployment of the **TruthLens S-09 Campaign** demonstrated that a structured intervention reduced student susceptibility by **62.1%** and raised active fact-checking behavior from **18.3% to 66.6%** ($p < 0.001$). 

To ensure long-term institutional resilience, this policy brief recommends four actionable institutional reforms.

---

## 2. Strategic Policy Recommendations

### Recommendation 1: Mandatory Digital Literacy & SIFT Module in First-Year Orientation
- **Action:** Integrate a mandatory 2-credit micro-course or 3-hour orientation workshop covering:
  - The SIFT verification methodology (Stop, Investigate, Find coverage, Trace context).
  - Reverse image and video forensic searching.
  - Phishing scam identification and academic integrity in the era of Generative AI.
- **Implementation Mechanism:** Delivered digitally via the University Learning Management System (LMS / Moodle) with automated completion certification.

---

### Recommendation 2: Establishment of a Student Fact-Checking & Media Ombudsman Desk
- **Action:** Form an independent, non-partisan student verification body within the Department of Journalism and Mass Communication.
- **Responsibilities:**
  1. Monitor and debunk viral campus rumors, fake circulars regarding exams/holidays, and financial scholarship hoaxes.
  2. Issue verified "Campus Truth Bulletins" across official university communication channels.
  3. Maintain the institutional TruthLens verification database.

---

### Recommendation 3: Academic Credit for Certified Peer Multipliers
- **Action:** Recognize student peer multipliers trained in digital forensics under the **National Service Scheme (NSS)** or Co-Curricular Activity (CCA) credit framework.
- **Impact:** Incentivizes senior undergraduates to mentor incoming cohorts and lead peer-to-peer verification workshops.

---

### Recommendation 4: Institutional Guidelines on Generative AI & Synthetic Media
- **Action:** Issue comprehensive university guidance on ethical AI usage, watermarking student-created synthetic media, and penalizing malicious deepfake generation or impersonation.

## 4. Conclusion & Motion for Adoption
Misinformation is a public health and civic literacy challenge. By institutionalizing media literacy and embedding the SIFT framework across the curriculum, the University can lead higher education in cultivating critically thinking digital citizens. We respectfully request the Academic Council to table this motion for formal adoption in the upcoming academic senate.
