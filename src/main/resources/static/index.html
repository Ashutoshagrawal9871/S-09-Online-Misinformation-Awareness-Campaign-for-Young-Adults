The challenge

A forward is faster than a fact-check.

Young adults meet most news inside private group chats and short-form feeds, where context is stripped away and urgency is engineered. A single forwarded voice note can empty a clinic, trigger a scam, or deepen a communal divide. The gap is not intelligence — it is a repeatable verification habit.

TruthLens (CUSoC S-09) closes that gap with a six-week campus campaign: two hands-on masterclasses, student peer multipliers, a public fact-check resource guide, and a website that turns the SIFT method into a two-minute routine.

Health myths

Miracle cures and anti-vaccine forwards delay real treatment and put families at risk.

Phishing & scams

Fake scholarships and subsidy links harvest OTPs, bank credentials, and personal data.

Deepfakes & AI

Synthetic voices and face swaps make manipulated clips look like first-hand evidence.

The method

Four moves. Two minutes. Better decisions.

SIFT is a lateral-reading routine developed by media literacy researcher Mike Caulfield. It replaces “does this feel true?” with a repeatable check.

Try it on a claim
S

Stop

Notice the emotional spike — fear, anger, urgency. Pause before liking, commenting, or forwarding.

Ask: why is this reaching me right now?

I

Investigate the source

Look up the domain, author, publication date, and whether the account actually exists.

Check: .gov.in / known newsroom / anonymous page?

F

Find better coverage

Search the claim itself. Prefer IFCN-certified fact-checkers and primary documents over reposts.

Try: PIB Fact Check, BOOM Live, Alt News

T

Trace claims

Reverse-search the image or video. Find the original upload, date, and full context.

Use: Google Lens, TinEye, InVID-WeVerify

Questions

Frequently asked

Short answers for evaluators, students, and anyone opening the project for the first time.

What is SIFT, and why this framework?

SIFT stands for Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, and Trace claims. It is a lateral-reading routine created by Mike Caulfield. It is fast enough for a group chat and teaches a habit rather than a one-off fact.

Is the fact-check verdict produced by AI?

No. The demo engine uses a transparent heuristic knowledge base that matches claim patterns to known misinformation categories. It returns a verdict, confidence score, SIFT steps, and trusted source suggestions. The architecture is ready for a live IFCN fact-check API, but no AI model is making the call.

How was the campaign impact measured?

Baseline and endline surveys used the same susceptibility items. The analysis reports a reduction from 7.4/10 to 2.8/10 (−62.1%, p < 0.001) and a +48.3% shift in verification behaviour. Reach, workshop attendance, and multiplier counts are documented in the social analytics and workshop reports.

Do I need an account to use the site?

No. The analyzer, modules, quiz, workshop listing, and resources are public. Registration for workshops only asks for a name, email, and college. The API also exposes JWT-based register and login endpoints for future authenticated features.

Where is data stored during the demo?

By default the app uses an in-memory H2 database seeded at startup, so demo data resets when the process restarts. The same code runs on PostgreSQL through environment variables for a persistent deployment. No sensitive personal data is required for the public features.

Can I reuse the campaign materials?

Yes. The repository is licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0. You can adapt the posters, scripts, and guides for non-commercial or commercial use as long as you credit TruthLens S-09 and share adaptations under the same license.
