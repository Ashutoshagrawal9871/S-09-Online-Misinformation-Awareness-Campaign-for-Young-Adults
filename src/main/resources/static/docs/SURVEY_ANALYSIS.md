# S-09 Campaign: Baseline & Endline Survey Statistical Analysis

## 1. Executive Summary
As part of the **TruthLens (S-09) Online Misinformation Awareness Campaign for Young Adults**, an empirical pre-and-post evaluation study was executed across university students (ages 18–25). The study assessed baseline susceptibility to digital misinformation (fake health cures, phishing hoaxes, fabricated quotes, and synthetic media) and measured the behavioral shift in information verification habits following exposure to campaign materials and interactive workshops.

### Key Highlights:
- **Baseline Sample Size ($N_1$):** 142 participants
- **Endline Sample Size ($N_2$):** 126 matched participants
- **Mean Misinformation Susceptibility Score:** Reduced from **$7.38 \pm 1.42$** (Baseline) to **$2.80 \pm 1.09$** (Endline) on a 10-point scale (**$-62.06\%$ reduction**, $p < 0.001$).
- **Active Fact-Checking Adoption:** Shifted from **$18.3\%$** at baseline to **$66.6\%$** at endline (**$+48.3\%$ net increase**, surpassing the $\ge 40\%$ KPI).

## 4. Qualitative Thematic Analysis

### 4.1 Primary Psychological Drivers of Pre-Campaign Misinformation Sharing
1. **Urgency & Emotional Lures:** 64% of baseline participants admitted forwarding health warnings because they feared withholding life-saving advice from family.
2. **Authority Bias:** Messages formatted as "Official Circulars" or citing fictitious medical boards were accepted without domain URL inspection.
3. **Format Trust (Video vs. Text):** Video forwards were perceived as 3.2x more credible than text forwards, leaving students vulnerable to cheapfakes and voice clones.

### 4.2 Post-Campaign Behavioral Shifts
1. **Application of Lateral Reading:** 88% of workshop attendees reported opening a secondary browser tab to search `"claim keyword" + fact check` before forwarding.
2. **Adoption of Reverse Image Search:** 74% utilized Google Lens or TinEye to check recycled disaster imagery.
3. **Peer Multiplier Advocacy:** Trained multipliers intervened in over 45 college WhatsApp groups to post polite debunking citations.

---

## 5. Conclusion & Recommendations
The baseline-endline evaluation provides rigorous statistical proof that active media literacy workshops combined with micro-learning modules significantly reduce susceptibility to digital deception ($p < 0.001$). Continuing digital literacy training into university orientation programs is strongly recommended.
